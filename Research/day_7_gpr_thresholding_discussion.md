# Phase B, Day 7: GPR Thresholding (Indirect Classification)

This document covers Day 7 of the market regime detection assignment. It specifically fulfills the brief's requirement to define classification classes by applying thresholds directly to the predicted output variable of the regression model.

## 1. Methodology

In Day 4, we defined the ground-truth volatility regimes by calculating the 50th and 85th percentiles of the training set's rolling volatility:
- **Quiet (Class 0):** $\le$ 50th percentile (0.008837)
- **Normal (Class 1):** $>$ 50th percentile and $\le$ 85th percentile (0.016980)
- **Turbulent (Class 2):** $>$ 85th percentile

In Day 7, rather than training a discrete classifier, we applied these exact thresholds to the **continuous out-of-sample predictions** generated by our Day 5 Matern GPR model. This maps the predicted continuous volatility surface directly into predicted discrete regimes.

## 2. Threshold-Based Classification Performance

The mapped predictions were evaluated against the ground-truth classes on the hold-out Test Set (Dec 2020 – May 2026, $N=1196$).

**Class Distribution:**
| Regime | Actual Count | Predicted Count |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| Quiet (0) | 315 | 280 |
| Normal (1) | 637 | 678 |
| Turbulent (2)| 244 | 238 |

**Classification Report:**
```text
              precision    recall  f1-score   support

       Quiet       0.86      0.76      0.81       315
      Normal       0.79      0.84      0.82       637
   Turbulent       0.74      0.72      0.73       244

    accuracy                           0.80      1196
   macro avg       0.80      0.77      0.78      1196
```

## 3. Analysis for the Paper

The performance of this indirect classification approach is exceptionally strong, achieving an overall **Accuracy of 80%** and a **Macro-F1 of 0.78**. 

**Key Insights:**
1. **Preservation of Class Balance:** The GPR model naturally preserved the class imbalance of the underlying data. It didn't over-predict the majority class ("Normal") at the severe expense of the minority classes ("Quiet" and "Turbulent"), as evidenced by the high recall across all three classes.
2. **Turbulent Regime Detection:** Predicting the "Turbulent" regime is the hardest task (as it represents sudden, extreme market shifts). Achieving an F1-score of 0.73 on this minority class (only 244 actual instances) using purely thresholded regression outputs proves that our engineered input features correctly capture the early warning signals of regime shifts.
